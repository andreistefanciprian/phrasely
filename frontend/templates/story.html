Why I built Phrasely

Connect on LinkedIn →

I'm Cip (pronounced Chip), from Romania.

I left in 2013 and have lived in six countries since, eventually settling in Australia. English was never my mother tongue, but wanting to communicate clearly and articulate my thoughts has been a constant.

For years I collected phrases.

Interesting expressions from podcasts. A clever sentence from a newspaper article. A word that made me stop and think, "I wish I could use that naturally."

Some went into a notebook. Some into spreadsheets. Some into a giant note on my phone. Some were carefully written down with context. Others were just a few words scribbled in a hurry while listening to a podcast on the way to work.

The collection grew into hundreds of entries.

The problem was that collecting wasn't learning.

Most of the phrases never became part of my vocabulary. Months later, I'd look at an entry and have no idea why I'd saved it. I had created an archive, not a learning system.

Then I started using AI to explain phrases, add context, identify the important word, and help me practise it. Suddenly things started to stick. A forgotten note became a memorable expression. A single word became a story. A phrase became something I could actually use.

But every conversation started from scratch. Nothing carried over.

So I built Phrasely.

A place where rough notes become memorable language. Paste a phrase, a fragment, or even a single word, and AI helps uncover what makes it worth remembering.

These days I rarely add phrases through the app itself. Most of that happens right inside ChatGPT, where I review, learn, understand, and add new phrases without ever leaving the conversation. Sometimes I ask it to weave phrases from my collection into what we're talking about. Other times I just ask it to pick a few at random and practice them with me through the chat.

I still open Phrasely itself too, mostly Shuffle and occasionally the Bubble page to see where I stand with using and reviewing my phrases. Shuffle especially has become my replacement for social media, a few idle minutes spent flipping through my own phrases instead of doom scrolling someone else's feed.

I built it for myself. But it isn't just for people learning English. It's for anyone who's ever heard a great expression and thought: "I want that to be part of my vocabulary."
